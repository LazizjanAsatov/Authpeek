Privacy policy

Last updated 19 October 2026.

What AuthPeek stores

The secret keys, names and linked websites of the accounts you add, plus your settings. Before anything is saved, the accounts are encrypted in your browser with AES-256-GCM, using a key made from your password (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds). Your password itself is never stored.

Where it goes

  • The encrypted accounts are saved with Chrome's sync storage. If you're signed in to Chrome with sync turned on, Google carries that encrypted data to your other computers. Google can't read it without your password.
  • While the extension is unlocked, the encryption key stays in Chrome's memory and is erased when it locks or Chrome closes. If you choose never to be asked for your password, the key is kept in this computer's Chrome profile (never synced) until you lock AuthPeek or change that setting.
  • Backup files are created on your computer, encrypted with your password. You decide where they go.

What it never does

  • It has no servers. Nothing is sent to the developer or any third party.
  • No analytics, tracking, ads or selling of data.
  • It never gives a code to a website you haven't linked to that account.

Why it asks for each permission

  • Read and change data on websites: to notice a verification-code box on a login page and fill the code in. The extension only looks at form fields; it doesn't read or save page content.
  • Storage: to keep your encrypted accounts and settings.
  • Active tab: to scan a QR code on the page you're looking at, only when you click Scan.

Removing your data

Delete accounts in the extension, or uninstall it. Uninstalling removes the data from this computer; to remove the synced copy as well, delete the accounts first or clear synced data in your Google account settings.
