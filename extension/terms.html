1. What AuthPeek is

AuthPeek is a free browser extension for Chrome that keeps two-step verification (TOTP) keys, shows their codes, and can fill them into sign-in pages you link. It runs entirely in your browser; the developer runs no servers and has no access to your accounts, keys or codes.

2. Your password and your keys are your responsibility

  • Your AuthPeek password encrypts your keys. It cannot be reset or recovered by anyone. If you forget it, your accounts in AuthPeek are lost.
  • Keep the recovery codes each website gives you when you turn on two-step verification, and keep a backup of AuthPeek (Settings → Backup). Losing access to your keys without these can lock you out of your accounts.
  • Anyone who uses your unlocked browser, or knows your AuthPeek password, can use your codes. Choose a strong password and a lock timer that fits how you use your computer.
  • Only add keys for accounts you own or are authorized to use.

3. Automatic filling and "Press Verify"

Filling a code, and pressing a site's Verify button for you, are conveniences. A site can reject a code (for example if a key in AuthPeek is out of date, or your computer's clock is wrong), and repeated wrong codes can make a site lock your account. AuthPeek fills a code by itself at most once per account and site every 10 minutes, and after that only suggests it. That limit reduces the risk but cannot remove it: each site decides its own rules. Check that your keys are current when a site rejects a code.

4. Sharing codes

A share link lets whoever has it see that account's codes for the minutes you chose (2, 5 or 10), and use them to sign in to that account during that time. It doesn't contain your secret key, so it stops working when those codes run out, but AuthPeek can't take a link back once you've sent it, or control who it is passed on to. Share only with people you trust, and only for accounts you're allowed to share. "Move to another app" hands over the secret key itself: whoever gets it can make your codes for good.

5. Whitelisted IPs

Whitelisted IPs keeps codes hidden off the networks you list, and refuses share links, backups and exports there too. It depends on Cloudflare's public service to learn this computer's public IP, and on an internal address you choose. It is a guardrail, not a security guarantee: someone who knows your AuthPeek password can move your keys to another app, and an internal address that also opens outside your network makes the list pass everywhere. If the check can't run, codes stay hidden, which can stop you from signing in until you're on a listed network or change the list with your password.
